# Market-clearing prices and the GIC

Chapter 8 of Cockshott and Cottrell's *Towards a New Socialism* (TNS) sells consumer goods at market-clearing prices, in labour tokens, while marking each good with its labour value. Each price is a markup on, or a discount from, the value. This note asks what that would do to the GIC's model in the *Fundamental Principles*. It follows Patrick's view that the two are compatible, provided both the labour time and the offset are recorded.

This is a research note by Claude (Opus 5.5), written from prompts by Patrick. Points marked *my reading* or *my illustration* are interpretation or construction, not something the sources say. Quotations from the GIC are from the [1935 edition in this repository](../Documents/fundamental_principles/README.md) unless the [1930 edition](fundamental-principles-1930-and-1935.md) is named.

## Patrick's prompts

> I'm fairly sure the GIC say vouchers have to have an expiry date.

## Summary

- **The accounting is compatible.** If the markup or discount is applied only when goods pass to consumers, every operational unit is still credited with its (f + c) + l, and nothing is gained or lost in its books. Markups and discounts form one separate account. In total it equals Token Consumption minus Consumption, and it can be settled through the next period's payout factor. Patrick's point stands.
- **TNS's net tax is its payout factor.** Both cancel the share of claims that goes to social provision and accumulation, and TNS also uses its tax to correct an imbalance in the consumer market. They differ in form. The GIC's factor is proportional and calculated from the accounts. TNS's tax is a flat amount per earner, set in advance from a forecast, with the share for accumulation put to a vote. TNS rejects turnover tax for the GIC's own reason, but it allows an excise tax on selected goods, which is price policy in the GIC's sense.
- **Both books already limit consumers with a scalar.** TNS's argument for prices is that free choice within a limit needs "a scalar (any goods you like up to such a total value) rather than a vector" ([TNS 15](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/15_Some_Contrary_Views_Considered.md#distribution-values-and-prices)). The GIC's certificates already do this. The two books differ on one thing only: whether the price a consumer pays may depart from the labour time.
- **The GIC rejects price policy by name.** Its objection is political, not about accounting. Whoever sets prices controls distribution, and only a fixed relation between producer and product prevents that. Recording both figures answers the GIC's other objection (that a surcharge hides how much work a product takes). It doesn't answer this one.
- **The GIC has no answer of its own to shortages within a plan period.** It accepts that needs won't be met at first, and it forbids units to expand beyond the plan. So a shortage stays a shortage until the next plan. TNS's markup is one answer to that gap. Rationing or queues are the other answers (*my reading*).
- **Hour for hour holds only on average.** With prices that vary, one certificate hour buys more or less than an hour of social labour depending on the good. The difference is a transfer between consumers according to their tastes.
- **Expiry is TNS's rule, not the GIC's.** TNS gives tokens a "use by" date and lets people save only through deposits the planners can monitor. Neither English edition of the *Fundamental Principles* in this collection mentions expiry. Expiry is needed if prices are to move, because otherwise people can hold tokens back to wait for a discount.
- **A version the GIC could accept would need five conditions:** offsets only at the point of distribution to consumers, both figures published, prices set by a published rule that the cooperatives apply, an expiry date on certificates, and a net balance that is never spent.

## What TNS proposes

Each consumer good carries two numbers. Its labour value is "the total amount of social labour which is required to produce [it], both directly and indirectly". Its price, in labour tokens, is set "as far as possible, at market-clearing levels". The book's example: a good of 10 hours that has excess demand at 10 tokens is raised to 12, so its ratio is 1.2 ([TNS 8](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md)).

The algorithm ([TNS 8, Consumer goods and the macro plan](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md#consumer-goods-and-the-macro-plan)):

1. A central marketing authority (CMA) orders consumer goods with a total labour content equal to the planned consumption.
2. It sells them at market-clearing prices.
3. For each good, it compares price/value with Token Consumption/Consumption. It orders more of a good with a higher ratio, the same of a good with an equal ratio, and less of a good with a lower ratio.
4. Repeat.

Over time this moves each price towards its value, so a markup or discount is meant to be temporary: a signal, not a permanent tax or subsidy. TNS keeps this pricing out of enterprises. There are no factor markets, enterprises are "not subjects of right", and the ratio is not a profit, because it "is not sensitive to the degree of exploitation" ([TNS 8, Comparison with capitalist markets](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md#comparison-with-capitalist-markets)). Chapter 9 also uses price minus value, averaged over the consumer goods an input goes into, to flag producer-goods enterprises for "further detailed study". This is evidence for a closer look, not a basis for pay ([TNS 9](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/09_Planning_and_Information.md#statistical-assessment-for-producer-goods-enterprises)).

TNS adds that market prices are right only where incomes are roughly equal. Where incomes are unequal and goods are scarce, rationing is better ([TNS 8, Table 8.1](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md#market-clearing-prices)). The GIC's equal hour meets that condition.

For more on the rule and its symbols, see [the economic model of TNS, E18 and E19](towards-a-new-socialism-economic-model.md#the-consumer-market-ch-89).

## Where it fits the GIC's books

### Operational units are untouched

In the GIC, an operational unit "only records how much social work it has taken from society in the form of f, c, and living labor and returns the *same amount* to society, but in a *different form*". It "*cannot*, therefore, have 'surpluses' or 'deficits'" ([GIC 11 d](../Documents/fundamental_principles/11_Accounting_as_Summary_of_Production_and_Distribution.md)). A markup applied only when the cooperative hands a good to a consumer never reaches the producing unit's books. The unit is credited with the production time through the giro office, whatever the good later fetches. Transfers between units stay at production time, as in both books.

### The offsets form one account

At the cooperative, each sale records two figures: the hours of product handed over (the value) and the certificates cancelled (the price). The difference, summed over all goods, is one account. TNS shows that the weighted average of price/value across all goods "will identically equal the macroeconomic ratio of Token Consumption to Consumption" ([TNS 8](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md#consumer-goods-and-the-macro-plan)). So the account's balance is just the gap between certificates spent and hours of product handed over.

The GIC already has an instrument for that gap. Its payout factor (FIC) is set so that the certificates issued match the product left for individual consumption ([GIC 10 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#g-the-payout-factor); [E7 in the GIC model note](fundamental-principles-economic-model.md#e7-the-payout-factor-fic)). A net balance in the offset account is carried into the next period's FIC calculation, either as product handed out and not paid for, or as certificates cancelled with nothing handed out.

### TNS's tax does the payout factor's job

TNS doesn't use the term "payout factor". Its net tax does the same job. In both books, workers can't be given claims on the whole of what they produce, because part of the product goes to social provision and accumulation. TNS: "consumption must fall short of Net Value Product, and therefore must also fall short of Current Labour Tokens … One way to arrange for this is to tax the labour-token income of the workers". Its example: if the plan gives 55 per cent of the Net Value Product to consumer goods and nobody saves, "the state must 'cancel out', via net taxation, 45 percent of the basic issue of labour tokens" ([TNS 7, Macro accounting in labour-time](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/07_Macroeconomic_Planning_and_Budgetary_Policy.md#macro-accounting-in-labour-time)). In GIC terms, that is a payout factor of 0.55 (*my reading*). TNS also gives the tax the job this note gives the payout factor: "tax policy will play the important role of balancing the macro plan". If Token Consumption overshoots Consumption, the planners "may respond in the next period by raising taxation" ([TNS 7, Tax policy](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/07_Macroeconomic_Planning_and_Budgetary_Policy.md#tax-policy); [TNS 8](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md#consumer-goods-and-the-macro-plan)).

So the offset account closes the same way in both books: through the GIC's next payout factor, or through TNS's next net tax. The two instruments do differ:

| | GIC payout factor | TNS net tax |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When it applies | Before issue: each hour is credited with FIC × 1 hour of certificates | After issue: one token per hour, then tax cancels part of them |
| Shape | Proportional: the same fraction of every hour | Flat: "so many labour tokens per month or year per earner". The marginal rate is zero, so an extra hour earns a whole token, and someone working short hours gives up a larger share |
| How it is set | Calculated from the period's accounts: the giro office "has in a simple way all the data necessary to determine the payout factor" ([GIC 10 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#g-the-payout-factor)) | Set in advance from the plan: revenue of *x* − *z* tokens, where *z* is forecast net saving. The share for accumulation is put to a plebiscite: "all tax levels must flow from democratic votes" |
| What it covers | Public (GSW) units, and expansion ([GIC 13 e](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#e-the-influence-on-the-payout-factor)) | Social provision, accumulation, lending abroad, and tokens transferred to non-workers |
| Saving | Not discussed | Part of the calculation: saving deposits and retirement assets reduce the tax needed |

For this note the difference that matters is the third row. The GIC's factor is a calculation from the accounts, so an offset balance would enter it automatically and openly. TNS's tax is a forecast and a vote, so a balance would be one input among several. Either way it is the same order of correction, a share of all hours, not a price on particular goods (*my reading*).

### A worked period (*my illustration*)

A cooperative receives two goods, each 100 units at 10 hours: 2,000 hours of product. Consumers hold 2,000 hours of certificates after the payout factor.

| Case | Good A | Good B | Certificates spent | Offset account | What follows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prices at value | 100 × 10 | 100 × 10 | 2,000 | 0 | Nothing. Units credited 1,000 hours each. |
| A scarce, B unpopular | 100 × 12 | 100 × 8 | 2,000 | +200 − 200 = 0 | Units still credited 1,000 each. Next period: order more A, less B. |
| B discounted too far | 100 × 12 | 100 × 7 | 1,900 | +200 − 300 = −100 | 100 certificates are left with no product to buy. If they expire, the balance closes. If not, next period's FIC must allow for them. |

In every case the producing units' books are the same. Only the offset account and the FIC see the prices.

### The two signals complement each other

The GIC links production to needs through orders: "the cooperatives are directly linked to the operational units, the needs, as they are expressed in the cooperatives, are *directly* transferred to production" ([GIC 12 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#g-the-cooperatives-and-the-benchmark-of-needs)). A sector that can't meet its orders "will make proposals for above-average expansion when the next production plan is drawn up" ([GIC 14 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)).

An order book shows *that* a good ran short, not *how much* people want it. The price/value ratio shows the strength of demand, and could back an expansion request within the GIC's limit on growth ([GIC 13 c–d](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#c-the-general-rule-for-the-expansion-of-the-operational-unit)) (*my reading*). The GIC's own control is reproduction measured against the social average ([GIC 14 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)). TNS's chapter 9 test is a second check of the same kind, and it adds information without touching anyone's credit.

### The GIC already tolerates prices that are off value, as error

The GIC expects production times to be wrong at first. In the 1930 edition, when certificates replace money, all products get a reproduction time "arrived at by sheer rule of thumb. This will inevitably mean that in one case it will be estimated too high, in another too low" ([1930 edition, appendix, PDF p. 206](../PDFs/FUNDAMENTAL%20PRINCIPLES%20OF%20COMMUNIST%20PRODUCTION%20AND%20DISTRIBUTION%20%281930%29.pdf)). In 1935 a wrong social average shows up in the accounts, because input and output no longer match ([GIC 14 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)). So a gap between what a consumer pays and the true labour time is nothing new in the GIC. What is new in TNS is that the gap is *deliberate*.

## Where it conflicts

### Price policy

The GIC's position is that the relation between producer and product must be fixed "in the things themselves". Then "there is no room for 'price policy'" ([GIC 9 e](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#e-the-wage-by-leichter)). The GIC treats this as the core demand of the revolution: "the exact relationship between producer and product" ([GIC 9 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#b-the-task-of-the-revolution)).

It rejects Varga's "social cost of production" even though Varga added the same percentage to *every* product ([GIC 10 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#c-the-usual-solution); [E4 in the GIC model note](fundamental-principles-economic-model.md#e4-leichters-surcharge-rejected)). It gives two reasons:

1. **A surcharge hides how much work each product takes.** It "never gives us a good overview of »*how much work each product requires to make it*«" ([GIC 10 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#c-the-usual-solution)). TNS answers this. Both figures are marked on the good, and the gap is the signal.
2. **Whoever sets prices controls distribution.** "Whoever has political power in the state also controls the entire social product and, through 'price policy', controls the distribution of national income" ([GIC 9 h](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#h-the-pricing-policy-in-hungary)). "The privileged classes will retreat to the fortress of price policy as the last position to maintain their privileges" ([GIC 10 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#c-the-usual-solution)). TNS doesn't answer this. Its markups differ from good to good, and a central body sets them "by trial and error, informed by the results of statistical analysis of demand elasticities". That is price policy in the GIC's sense. TNS's prices are meant to converge on value, but the authority still decides how fast and by how much.

There is a third objection, from the GIC's growth argument: "the 'social costs' [must] be determined by a payout factor and not by the detour of 'price increases' since this would directly limit self-activity" ([GIC 10 h](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#h-the-growth-process-of-communism)). This is aimed at funding public services through prices. It would apply to the offset account if the account were ever spent.

TNS agrees on this principle. It rejects the Soviet turnover tax because "it would involve systematically pricing consumer goods at values greater than their true labour content", and it prefers a tax on token income ([TNS 7, Tax policy](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/07_Macroeconomic_Planning_and_Budgetary_Policy.md#tax-policy)). That is the GIC's case against Varga in other words. But TNS makes two exceptions the GIC wouldn't:

- **An excise tax**, "a specific charge on selected consumer goods" (alcohol, tobacco) whose consumption the state wants to limit ([TNS 7, Excise tax](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/07_Macroeconomic_Planning_and_Budgetary_Policy.md#excise-tax)). Unlike the ch. 8 markup, this one is permanent and set by policy, not by demand. It is price policy in exactly the GIC's sense.
- **Ground rent paid in tokens**, which TNS calls "one exception to the general principle that these tokens buy only the products of labour, priced according to their labour content" ([TNS 7, Ground rent](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/07_Macroeconomic_Planning_and_Budgetary_Policy.md#ground-rent)).

So the ch. 8 markup is the milder of TNS's departures from labour-time pricing. It is temporary and driven by demand, and meant to converge on value. The excise tax is the one the GIC's objection fits fully (*my reading*).

### Shortages within a plan period

The GIC admits that at first "it will certainly not be possible to satisfy needs", and that units "are not supposed to expand production on their own authority … because they cannot go beyond the general framework established in the general production plan" ([GIC 12 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#g-the-cooperatives-and-the-benchmark-of-needs)). It doesn't say what the cooperative does when a good runs out before the period ends. With prices fixed at labour time, the possibilities are first come first served, queues, or rationing by the cooperative (*my reading*). TNS's ch. 15 makes the same point against a priceless system: without a price, "the practical result [will] simply be 'first come, first served'" ([TNS 15](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/15_Some_Contrary_Views_Considered.md#distribution-values-and-prices)). TNS's Table 8.1 prefers market prices to rationing when incomes are equal. The GIC doesn't consider the choice.

### Hour for hour holds only on average

Marx's principle, quoted by the GIC: the worker "draws from the social stock of means of consumption as much as the same amount of labor cost" ([GIC 9 i](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#i-fair-distribution)). With markups, a certificate hour buys less than an hour of social labour on popular goods and more on unpopular ones. The offset account nets out, but as a transfer *between consumers*. People who want what is scarce pay those who take what is left. TNS defends this as the "votes" of consumers with equal incomes ([TNS 8](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md#comparison-with-capitalist-markets)). Under it, the exact relation between producer and product holds for the whole basket of goods, not for each purchase.

### Saving and expiry

Prices that move give people a reason to hold on to tokens and wait. TNS counts on this to steady prices: consumers "may decide to postpone consumption of the item, in the anticipation of a lower price in future" ([TNS 8](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/08_The_Marketing_of_Consumer_Goods.md#consumer-goods-and-the-macro-plan)). Unspent tokens throw out the balance between Token Consumption and Consumption.

TNS limits this with an expiry date. From Marx's remark that labour money is no more money than a theatre ticket, TNS concludes that certificates "would not serve as a store of value. They could have a 'use by' date on them. Unless individuals redeemed their share of this year's output by the end of the year, it would be assumed that they did not want it" ([TNS 2](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/02_Eliminating_Inequalities.md)). In ch. 7, hoarding "is avoided by having the labour tokens expire after a specified date". Saving is allowed only through retirement assets and consumer deposits run by a state financial system, "so that their aggregate volume can be monitored" ([TNS 7](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/07_Macroeconomic_Planning_and_Budgetary_Policy.md#household-saving-and-credit)).

The GIC doesn't say this. Neither the 1935 edition (in the Markdown or its PDF) nor the 1930 edition mentions certificates expiring, their validity, or hoarding. The nearest passage is the 1930 appendix, where a set date is fixed after which old *money* "will be declared worthless and only labour certificates will give entitlement to social product" (PDF p. 206). The GIC builds its accounts on Marx's theatre ticket, as TNS does ([GIC 9 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#c-the-consumption-money)), but doesn't draw the expiry conclusion from it. The German and Dutch originals haven't been checked.

So expiry is part of the TNS design, not something the GIC already has. Without it, varying prices make the FIC absorb people's waiting for discounts as well as the offset balance. With it, waiting is possible only within the validity period, and lapsed certificates become one more balance for the FIC.

## Conditions for a version the GIC could accept

*My reading*, from the objections above:

1. **Offsets only at the point of distribution to consumers.** Units and transfers between units stay at production time. The ratio is never credited to a unit.
2. **Both figures marked on every good and published,** with the offset account public in the giro office's social accounts.
3. **Prices set by a published mechanical rule,** for example from stock levels against the period's order. The cooperatives apply it, not a central marketing authority. This is the GIC's answer to price policy: control by rules, not by persons ([GIC 13 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#c-the-general-rule-for-the-expansion-of-the-operational-unit) calls for "generally valid rules").
4. **An expiry date on certificates,** with saving only through accounts the giro office can see.
5. **The net balance is never spent.** It is used only to correct the next payout factor (in TNS's terms, the next net tax). Otherwise it becomes a hidden tax, the route of Leichter and Varga ([GIC 10 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#f-the-marxist-solution)).

With these conditions, the idea is an addition to the GIC's model, not something already in it. A strict GIC reader could still object that any deliberate departure from labour time breaks "the law of average production time" ([GIC 10 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#f-the-marxist-solution)). The reply would be that the cooperatives face shortages either way, and a published price rule is more open than an unwritten queue (*my reading*).
