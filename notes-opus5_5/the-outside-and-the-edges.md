# The outside and the edges

Where does a labour-time economy end? This note looks for its outside, beyond the books, and its edges, where labour or exchange slips out of them: slavery, domestic labour, cheating, money reinvented, the black market and old money, where certificates are kept, and sex work. It follows on from the simulation's decision that there is an outside, explicitly acknowledged ([gap 5](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics), [The key number](../plans/simulator/key-number.md)), and from [LETS, Parecon and the GIC](lets-parecon-and-one-country.md), which covers the outside in space (other countries) and in time (the inheritance).

This is a research note by Claude, written from a brief by Patrick. Points marked *my reading* are interpretation, not something the sources say. Points marked *not checked* are from memory and not verified for this note.

## Summary

- **The economy ends where the books end.** The GIC's boundary is the accounting, not a border: "those who are not members of the social accounting cannot receive raw materials" ([ch. 16](../Documents/fundamental_principles/16_The_Economic_Dictatorship_of_the_Proletariat.md)). So the outside is not only other countries and the past. It is also *inside*: every hour of labour that is done but not recorded, and every exchange that doesn't pass through the giro office.
- **What isn't counted isn't saved.** TNS's point about the ancients is not that slavery was unprofitable, but that a master who doesn't pay by the hour has no reason to count hours, and so no reason to save them ([TNS ch. 3](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/03_Work_Time_and_Computers.md#economies-of-time)). The same holds for every edge in this note. Labour off the books is both unrewarded and invisible to the one measure the system uses to economise.
- **Slavery in the Americas lasted about 350 years**, from the early 1500s to Brazil's abolition in 1888. Its overlap with *industrial* capitalism, from about the 1760s, is about 125 years, which fits Patrick's 100–150. Whether it ended because it was inefficient is the old Eric Williams thesis, and most later historians reject it: slavery was profitable when it was abolished, and it was ended by revolt, abolitionism and war. Today about 50 million people are in modern slavery.
- **Domestic labour is the largest outside by hours**, and women do about three-quarters of it (76.2 % of unpaid care hours worldwide, ILO 2018). TNS call it a "domestic economy", a mode of production of its own, marked by unpaid work and "highly mystified" exploitation ([TNS ch. 1](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/01_Inequality.md#womens-economic-subordination)). TNS credit housework only once it is collective: in their commune, kitchen work and factory work are paid at par ([TNS ch. 12](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/12_The_Commune.md#systems-of-payment-and-external-trade)). Housework in a family household stays unpaid; their answer is to replace it, not to pay it. The simulation pays child care, at home or at a centre ([gap 8](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics)); cooking, cleaning and the care of disabled people are brought in by [gaps 53 and 54](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics)).
- **There is no tax to evade, so cheating moves.** The deduction comes off at source, through the payout factor, before any certificate exists. What remains is working for something other than certificates, claiming hours not worked, misreporting a unit's books, and theft. The GIC's control is the public books and the social average ([ch. 14 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)), which they admit is weak for public units.
- **Money can be reinvented at any time, but it can't become capital** while means of production come only through the accounts. Certificates can't circulate, but goods can, and any durable, wanted good can become money (cigarettes in a prisoners' camp). The danger is where the outside and the edges meet: a unit that gets raw materials from the outside, for money.
- **Crypto changed who keeps the ledger, not what the ledger records.** Bitcoin is money without a bank: it circulates, it can be hoarded, and it can buy labour. The GIC's certificate is a record that doesn't circulate. The difference is the social relation, not the technology.
- **The black market is the outside inside.** TNS cover foreign currency, smuggling and drugs, but neither they nor the GIC say what happens to the old money in people's hands. Some of it is never handed in (12 billion Deutsche Marks still out after the euro). Held by former capitalists and opponents, and still honoured abroad, it is a ready-made currency for the black market. East Germany (1957) and Cuba (1961) swapped their notes by surprise to strip holdings abroad. *My reading:* convert personal holdings once, up to a limit, larger sums only on proof of origin, then never again.
- **Certificates belong in accounts, not in tokens.** One public accounting office or several federated ones; a card only as the key. A bearer token (paper, a stored-value chip, a crypto key) can be handed on, so it circulates. A **blockchain** solves agreement without a trusted keeper, which this economy has; its transferable tokens, proof of work and public ledger work against the design. Its useful part is the hash-chained, append-only log, which needs no blockchain.
- **Certificates can't pay a dealer directly**, since they go only to a store and a sale earns the seller nothing. The ways round are indirect: goods as payment, buying for the dealer or lending a card, ghost hours, diverted medicines, transfers abroad, and old money.
- **Sex work is not a public unit.** A public unit supplies what society budgets as a need, free. In my reading that can't fit sex, which can't be a social obligation to supply. In the GIC's categories it would be a productive unit (a workers' cooperative charging its production time), or it stays off the books. The GIC and TNS say nothing about it.

## Where the economy ends

*My reading.* There are three outsides, and the last is the one this note is about.

| Outside | What it is | Where it is covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In space** | Other economies, capitalist or not | [LETS, Parecon and the GIC](lets-parecon-and-one-country.md#the-outside-socialism-in-one-country); TNS [ch. 10](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/10_Foreign_Trade.md) |
| **In time** | Everything inherited: stocks, machines, an altered atmosphere | The key number ([17 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/17_Final_Considerations.md#c-the-key-number)); [The key number](../plans/simulator/key-number.md); [Carbon and labour time](carbon-and-labour-time.md) |
| **Inside** | Labour done and exchange made off the books | This note |

The GIC define the economy by its accounting. A unit is inside if its flows pass through the giro office, and that is the only way to get raw materials. A unit outside "excludes itself from the communist community". They call this "economic dictatorship", one "not carried out by bayonet, but by the economic laws of movement of communism", and expect it to die away as everyone joins ([ch. 16](../Documents/fundamental_principles/16_The_Economic_Dictatorship_of_the_Proletariat.md)).

That boundary is sharp for means of production and blurred for everything else. It says what a unit can't get. It says nothing about what a person does with their own time, their own goods, or each other.

## Slavery

### TNS on the ancients

TNS give the argument in chapter 3:

> Historians have long argued that the reason why the ancients failed to develop an industrial society, despite all the science of the Greeks and the engineering skills of the Romans, was the institution of slavery. Where all industrial production was relegated to slaves, rational calculation of labour costs was discouraged. A slave was not paid by the hour, so the master had no incentive to account for the hours of his servants' labour.

They go on: "Capitalism was a clear advance on slavery. The capitalist buys his labour by the hour and is reluctant to waste it ... But still, he buys his labour cheap", and cheap labour is squandered too. Their examples are the navvies who built Britain's railways with "the same tools as the Roman slaves who built the aqueducts", and dockers unloading by hand until "full employment, strong trades unions and better wages" made containers worth the investment ([TNS ch. 3](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/03_Work_Time_and_Computers.md#economies-of-time)).

So TNS's claim is about *calculation*, not profit. Slavery hid the cost of labour from the person who could have saved it. Wage labour shows it, but at a price below its value. Labour-time accounting shows it at full value, so every hour saved counts.

The ancient half of the argument is contested. TNS cite Lynn White (1962) for the Romans never applying water power widely. Archaeology since has found more than White knew of: the mills at Barbegal near Arles, sixteen overshot wheels in two rows of eight, are dated to the second century CE (one study argues the end of the third) and may have made about 25 tonnes of flour a day, probably ship's biscuit for the harbours ([Oxford, School of Archaeology](https://www.arch.ox.ac.uk/node/2406646); [Mainz](https://press.uni-mainz.de/the-hydraulics-of-the-worlds-first-industrial-plant-a-unique-construction-in-the-barbegal-water-mills/)). The Romans did apply water power, at least in places. Whether slavery held back its spread is still argued.

### Slavery and capitalism

Patrick's history, checked (*not checked* against primary sources for this note; these are standard dates):

- **How long.** Enslaved Africans were carried to the Americas from the early 1500s. Abolition came colony by colony: Haiti by revolution (1791–1804), the British Empire by the Act of 1833 (in force 1834, with "apprenticeship" until 1838), the United States by the Thirteenth Amendment (1865), Cuba (1886), and Brazil last (1888). So about 350 years in all. Industrial capitalism, dated from the 1760s, overlaps it by about 125 years.
- **Part of capitalism.** Marx was clear that it was: "the veiled slavery of the wage-workers in Europe needed, for its pedestal, slavery pure and simple in the new world" (*Capital*, vol. 1, ch. 31). Lancashire's mills ran on slave-grown cotton. In 1846 he wrote that "without slavery no cotton; without cotton no modern industry" (letter to Annenkov).
- **Ended by its inefficiency?** That is the thesis of Eric Williams's *Capitalism and Slavery* (1944): the British plantation economy was in decline, and abolition followed the money. Seymour Drescher's *Econocide* (1977) argued the opposite for Britain: the slave economy was growing when Parliament ended it, so abolition cost Britain dearly. For the United States, Fogel and Engerman's *Time on the Cross* (1974) found slave agriculture profitable and, by their measures, efficient; that finding was disputed, but the profitability largely stood. On this view slavery was not out-competed. It was abolished, by the enslaved themselves (Haiti, and countless revolts), by abolitionist movements, and by war.

*My reading.* The two views can be joined by TNS's argument. Slavery could be profitable and still be a brake on productivity, because profit can come from the cheapness of labour rather than from saving it. That is TNS's point about the navvies too. What wage labour brought was not a more efficient master but a labour cost that could be counted by the hour.

### Slavery today

The ILO, Walk Free and the IOM estimate that on an average day in 2021, **49.6 million people** were in modern slavery: 27.6 million in forced labour and 22 million in forced marriage, about 10 million more than in 2016 ([ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/50-million-people-worldwide-modern-slavery-0); [Walk Free](https://www.walkfree.org/reports/global-estimates-of-modern-slavery-2022/)). That is about one person in 150, not a minor matter, though small beside the 3.5 billion who work.

### In the GIC's books

*My reading.* Forced labour is labour whose hours are taken and not credited. In the GIC's books every hour worked is credited to the person who worked it, so a unit using unrecorded labour shows a production time below what its recorded hours could produce. The GIC's control works through the social average: an operation "well above the average" prompts "an investigation of the causes" ([14 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)). They don't say the same of an operation well *below* it. They should: an improbably low production time is the sign of hidden labour, as an improbably high one is the sign of waste.

## Domestic labour

TNS give it more space than any other text in this repository. In their first chapter it is one of the four sources of inequality, beside exploitation, unemployment and old age:

> The domestic economy is marked by the unpaid performance of labour services in the family. The majority of those who directly produce these services are married women; the non-producers who get the benefits of these labour services are children and husbands. It is another feature of the domestic economy that neither the results of work nor the work itself is paid for.

They call it a mode of production of its own, which has lost ground to capitalism (food, cloth, milling) and to public services (school, care of the old), and whose exploitation is "highly mystified. Cloaked as it is in the ideologies of sexual and maternal love, people tend to overlook it" ([TNS ch. 1](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/01_Inequality.md#womens-economic-subordination)). Patrick's "akin to slavery" is stronger than TNS's word, which is exploitation; what the two share is that neither the hours nor the product enter any book.

Their answer is the urban commune ([TNS ch. 12](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/12_The_Commune.md#systems-of-payment-and-external-trade)): 50 to a couple of hundred adults sharing housing, kitchens, child care and help for the old. Members are paid in internal work-units, "one work-unit per hour regardless of whether they are working inside the commune or in the national economy", and the commune converts them to national labour tickets. So TNS *do* credit housework, but only when it is done collectively, in a commune. They don't propose paying the cook in a family kitchen. The domestic economy is to be replaced, not paid, and voluntarily: "we do not envisage compulsory collectivisation ... Communes will flourish only if they can demonstrate their desirability as an alternative to bourgeois family life." Until then, housework in a family stays off the books.

Who does it: globally, women perform **76.2 % of the hours of unpaid care work**, more than three times as much as men; in Asia and the Pacific, 80 % ([ILO, *Care work and care jobs for the future of decent work*, 2018](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-calls-urgent-action-prevent-looming-global-care-crisis)). In 2018, 606 million women of working age said unpaid care kept them out of the labour force, against 41 million men.

**Care of disabled people.** TNS list "part of the care of the elderly and infirm" among the domestic economy's remaining fields (ch. 1), and they credit disabled people themselves with labour tokens from the state budget ([TNS ch. 7](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/07_Macroeconomic_Planning_and_Budgetary_Policy.md)). They say nothing about the people who care for them. The GIC say nothing at all. A profoundly disabled child is the hardest case: the care may be lifelong, may never pass to a school, and may not be timeable. It falls on the household, mostly on women, and in the GIC's scheme it is the "great injustice" at its sharpest ([9 i](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#i-fair-distribution)). The simulation's answer is [gap 53](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics).

The GIC say almost nothing. Their split between productive and public units leaves the household out, and they accept that people with more dependants are worse off as a "great injustice" of the first phase ([9 i](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#i-fair-distribution)). The [general social work note](general-social-work.md#unpaid-domestic-and-care-work) has the size of domestic labour, the 2026 German debate on paying certificates for home care, and an assessment: the arithmetic of paying it is sound; the real question is control and measurement.

The simulation has taken the middle route. Child carers, at the centre or at home, are paid as public work, through a child care unit with a budget ([gap 8](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics)). Cooking, cleaning and the rest of the housework stay off the books, and so does the care of disabled people beyond what child care covers. Both are decided in [gaps 53 and 54](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics).

## The edges: cheating

### What there is to evade

Under capitalism the "shadow economy" is large. Medina and Schneider's study for the IMF puts it at an average of 31.9 % of official GDP across 158 countries over 1991–2015, from 7.2 % in Switzerland to 62.3 % in Bolivia ([IMF Working Paper 18/17](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2018/01/25/Shadow-Economies-Around-the-World-What-Did-We-Learn-Over-the-Last-20-Years-45583)). Most of it is hiding income from tax and labour from regulation.

In the GIC's scheme there is no tax and no income to hide. Social costs come off at source, through the payout factor ([10 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#g-the-payout-factor)), before a certificate exists, and a certificate is issued only for hours recorded. "No one can have an 'income' higher than the products he can take from consumption for the hours he works" ([14 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)). So the cheating changes form.

| Under capitalism | Under labour-time accounting | How it shows, if it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hiding income from tax | Working for something other than certificates: goods, favours, another money | It doesn't, unless the materials used were taken from a unit |
| Inflating expenses | Claiming hours not worked | Production time above the average; the unit's council knows who was there |
| Cooking the company's books | Misreporting a unit's f, c, l or output | Inputs and outputs don't match: "input and output power must always be the same" (14 b) |
| Theft | Theft, from a unit or a store | Stock that the books say is there and isn't |
| Smuggling | Goods across the gate off the books | Nothing in the books at all; TNS ch. 10 |
| Undeclared labour | Unrecorded labour, including forced labour | Production time *below* what the recorded hours could do |

The simulation already plans for the middle four: theft, false reports, feigned illness and shirking are a policy, off by default ([gap 45](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-simulation)), and the engine keeps what happened apart from what was reported. The first and the last are not yet in it.

### Where the pressure comes from

*My reading.* People cheat a system of equal hours for the same reasons they cheat any other: to get something they can't get, or more of it.

- **Shortage.** Where goods are allocated first come, first served ([gap 17](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics)), a queue is a market waiting to happen. The Soviet "second economy" grew in the gaps of a shortage economy (Gregory Grossman, "The 'Second Economy' of the USSR", 1977, *not checked*). The pull system's targets and the speed with which units respond decide how long queues last.
- **Goods society chooses not to supply.** TNS's example is drugs, bought with foreign currency, and their answer is non-transferable accounts and a ban on importing currency: "In the absence of cash how could street deals for drugs be paid for?" They add: "We should not underestimate the ability of criminals and petty capitalists to devise new means of payment" ([TNS ch. 10](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/10_Foreign_Trade.md#exchange-rates-tourism-and-black-markets)).
- **Personal services and small trades.** TNS would allow self-employment, for plumbing and repairs, through a trading account with the state bank, and note that such trades are where the "black economy" of the former socialist countries was "notorious" ([TNS ch. 14](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/14_Property_Relations.md#self-employment)). In the GIC's terms a self-employed plumber is an operational unit of one, inside the accounts like any other.
- **Weak control of public units.** The GIC admit it: public units have no social average, and accounting can only check that a service stays within its budget, not "whether the 'service' is sufficiently productive" (14 b).

## Money reinvented

### What can and can't circulate

Marx's certificate is "no more 'money' than a ticket for the theatre", and the GIC quote him ([9 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#c-the-consumption-money)). TNS make it explicit: labour credits are non-transferable and cancelled on use, "like train tickets"; individuals "could not hire other individuals to work for them because of the non-transferability of labour credits" ([TNS ch. 14](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/14_Property_Relations.md#rights-of-planning-and-economic-projects)).

But goods are transferable. Once a person has bought something, they can give it, swap it or sell it. Any durable, divisible, widely wanted good can become money: cigarettes became the currency of a Second World War prisoners' camp, with prices, credit and inflation (R. A. Radford, "The Economic Organisation of a P.O.W. Camp", *Economica*, 1945, *not checked*). So **money can be reinvented at any time, among consumers**. The question is what it can buy.

*My reading.* In the GIC's scheme it can buy:

- **Second-hand goods** and anything made at home from consumer goods.
- **Personal services**: repairs, care, sex, lessons. Here a reinvented money can hire labour, so here the old servant relation can come back: someone with goods to spare paying someone else, in goods, to do their housework.

It can't buy means of production on any scale, because raw materials and machines come only through the accounts (ch. 16). A reinvented money stays petty unless it can reach them. It reaches them in one of three ways:

1. **A unit sells on the side.** It must hide output, which shows as inputs without matching outputs (14 b).
2. **A unit leaves the accounts and finds another supplier.** In a closed communist world there is none. **With an outside that trades in money, there is one.** This is where the outside and the edges meet, and why TNS ban the import of currency and let labour credits circulate only abroad ([TNS ch. 10](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/10_Foreign_Trade.md#alternatives-to-foreign-exchange)). The simulation's gate takes no money after day one ([gap 52](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics)), which closes this door by decision.
3. **People choose it.** The GIC put the whole changeover this way: whether a new money or the working hour comes out of the revolution "is a decisive question of power" ([17 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/17_Final_Considerations.md#b-from-the-money-account-to-the-working-time-account), [17 e](../Documents/fundamental_principles/17_Final_Considerations.md#e-summary)). Fifty years on it would be the same question, asked again.

### What crypto thought was new

The Bitcoin paper (2008) proposed "a purely peer-to-peer version of electronic cash" that would let payments be made "without going through a financial institution", replacing trust in a third party with cryptographic proof (*not checked* against the paper for this note). The new thing was the ledger without a keeper. The supply was fixed, so holders expected it to rise in value against goods, and it was hoarded.

*My reading.* In the GIC's terms nothing about money's social relation changed. A bitcoin circulates, can be saved, and can buy labour; it is a general equivalent, with a new bookkeeper. Its promise of freedom from banks and states was freedom for the holder of money, which is the freedom the GIC set out to abolish. The certificate is the opposite design: it has a keeper (the giro office, which could itself be distributed), but it records a contribution and is cancelled on use, so it can't circulate, can't be hoarded into capital, and can't hire. The line between them is not the technology. It is transferability.

### Sanctioned sub-currencies

TNS have one of their own. A commune pays its members in internal work-units, convertible into national labour tickets "at the going exchange rate". They show the rate stays at par only if "the commune must do its accounts correctly; it must run a balanced collective fund (on average); and it must budget for just as much non-basic internal labour as commune members demand" ([TNS ch. 12](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/12_The_Commune.md#systems-of-payment-and-external-trade)). *My reading:* that is a useful test for any reinvented unit. A local currency whose rate falls below par is a sign that something is mis-allocated; it is not harmless because it is local. LETS is the capitalist-world precedent ([LETS note](lets-parecon-and-one-country.md#lets)).

## The black market

The black market is the plainest name for the outside that sits inside: exchange that is forbidden, or that goes around the books. It is where the edges above meet. Reinvented money, goods bought and passed on, labour paid in goods, and the outside's money all trade there.

### What TNS cover, and what they leave out

TNS's chapter on foreign trade has the only extended treatment of the black market in this repository ([TNS ch. 10](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/10_Foreign_Trade.md#exchange-rates-tourism-and-black-markets)). It covers three things:

- **Foreign currency.** A black market in currency appears when the state's own rate overvalues the domestic currency, and the Soviet *beriozka* shops, selling goods only for hard currency, gave citizens a reason to want it. It "corrodes social values" and "creates a subculture of semi-criminal petty capitalists". The cure: state shops take only labour tokens.
- **Smuggling and drugs.** Citizens want foreign currency to buy what the state doesn't import, "cocaine, pornographic videos, etc.". Without cash, street deals are hard to pay for. What is left is foreign banknotes and gold, so the import of currency is prohibited and "all domestic purchases will already be made using plastic cards".
- **Travel.** A citizen abroad may turn labour credits into yen, but may not bring yen home, and a foreign bank may not transfer credits back to a citizen ([Alternatives to foreign exchange](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/10_Foreign_Trade.md#alternatives-to-foreign-exchange)). The flow is one way.

What TNS leave out is the money already inside the country on the day labour credits begin. So do the GIC: their conversion by the key number is of the operational units' books ([17 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/17_Final_Considerations.md#b-from-the-money-account-to-the-working-time-account), [17 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/17_Final_Considerations.md#c-the-key-number)). Neither says what becomes of the banknotes in people's drawers, or of bank deposits.

### Old money

*My reading*, except where a source is given.

Some of the old currency will never be handed in. People keep it for different reasons, and the reasons matter, because they decide what it can be used for.

| Who keeps it | Why | What it can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The nostalgic and the collector** | Keepsake, habit, forgetfulness | Nothing. It is a souvenir, like a coin from a holiday |
| **The cautious** | Insurance, in case the new system fails | A small store of value on the black market, priced by how likely people think a return to money is |
| **Former capitalists, and those who lost by the change** | Their savings, which the change made worthless or capped | A fund for the black market, and the means to pay others |
| **Opponents of the new society** | To finance work against it: bribes, propaganda, sabotage, hiring | The same, topped up from abroad, where the old currency may still be held in quantity |

The scale is real even in a peaceful changeover. When the euro replaced the Deutsche Mark in 2002, Germans could exchange marks for euros at no cost and with no deadline. More than twenty years on, the Bundesbank counts about 12 billion marks never handed in: some lost or destroyed, some kept, and tens of millions still exchanged each year ([Bundesbank, outstanding DM cash](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/618390/06edcc8c91b804001d1acd4285ad824e/472B63F073F071307366337C94F8C870/ausstehendes-dm-bargeld-data.pdf); [Bundesbank FAQ](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/tasks/cash-management/frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-dm-banknotes)). That is with every reason to hand it in.

Two socialist states replaced their banknotes partly to strip opponents abroad of their holdings:

- **East Germany, 13 October 1957.** Without warning, citizens had to exchange their notes one for one; bank deposits were untouched. The official aim was to make worthless the East marks that had flowed to West Berlin and West Germany, estimated at 50 to 100 million Deutsche Marks' worth. Anyone exchanging a large sum had to show it was lawfully acquired, which fell hardest on farmers, traders and the self-employed, and much cash was never presented, either because it was in the West or because its owners didn't want to be checked ([GHDI](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/occupation-and-the-emergence-of-two-states-1945-1961/interview-with-east-berliners-about-the-currency-reform-in-the-gdr-october-20-1957); [bpb](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutschlandarchiv/235558/der-waehrungsumtausch-als-marionettenspiel-greta-kuckhoff-der-geldumtausch-in-der-ddr-und-parteiliche-disziplinierung/)).
- **Cuba, 5–6 August 1961.** With Che Guevara at the National Bank, the notes were replaced over a weekend. The new ones had been printed in secret in Prague, so that the large stock of old notes held in the United States could not be sent back to be exchanged. Some Cubans burned their old money ([*Time*](https://time.com/archive/6810309/cuba-keeping-them-poor); [The E-Sylum](https://coinbooks.org/v27/esylum_v27n39a30.html)). An earlier law, in April 1959, had already withdrawn the 500 and 1,000 peso notes for fear they would be used against the new government.

Both were exchanges of one money for another. A change to labour certificates has a harder question to answer: should old money convert into certificates at all? A certificate is a claim on the product for work done. Converting savings would hand claims to people for hours they didn't work, and to some for hours others worked for them. Not converting them takes from small savers as well as from the rich. *My reading:* the GIC's logic points to a window, as in 1957 and 1961, in which personal holdings convert at the key number up to a limit, with larger sums converted only on proof of how they were come by, and nothing after the window closes. A limit spares the small saver and the pensioner; the proof is what the GDR asked for. Bank deposits are easier than cash, because they are already on a record.

After the window, old money is no longer money. The stores don't take it, and there is nothing in the books it can buy.

For Marx, money *is* a commodity: the one commodity set apart to serve as the general equivalent, in which all the others express their value (*Capital*, vol. 1, chs. 1–3, *not checked* against the text for this note). Gold was money and kept its use value as a metal: it still stopped teeth and made jewellery. Marx says the money commodity's use value becomes twofold, its use value as a metal and a "formal" use value from its function as money (ch. 2, *not checked*). A banknote is different. It has next to no value of its own, and Marx calls it a symbol of value, which circulates within one country because the state compels it to (ch. 3, "The Coin and Symbols of Value", *not checked*). When the state cancels it, the note loses its function and is left with almost nothing: paper, plus a use value for collectors. Unlike gold, an old note has no use to fall back on. What it can still fetch on the black market depends on two things:

1. **Whether it can buy from the outside.** If the old currency, or a foreign one, is still accepted abroad, then it is a door to the outside's goods, and to its means of production. This is TNS's reason to forbid importing currency, and it applies to the old national currency as much as to the dollar. A new society that cancels its old notes can't stop them being held abroad, as East Germany and Cuba found; what it can do is make sure no one at home will exchange them.
2. **Whether people expect it back.** A banknote of the old regime is a bet on its return. *My reading:* its black-market price is a rough gauge of how many people think the new society will fail. If it trades for more than paper, someone expects it to be good again.

That is the difference between old money and the reinvented money of the last section. Reinvented money grows out of shortage, and stays petty without the means of production. Old money comes with a constituency, with savings behind it, and with an outside that may still honour it. The danger is not the notes. It is that they give the former owning class, and anyone abroad who backs it, a currency ready-made.

## Where certificates are kept

### Beyond the simulation

In the simulation an account is a number in memory ([Data model](../plans/simulator/data-model.md)). A real system has to decide where the record lives and how a person proves it is theirs. The GIC put all accounting in one general giro office, the public accounting office in the simulation's terms ([ch. 16](../Documents/fundamental_principles/16_The_Economic_Dictatorship_of_the_Proletariat.md)). TNS have "plastic cards". Between them the choices are:

| Where | How it works | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **One public accounting office** | Every account is an entry in one set of books. A card, phone or signature only proves who is spending; the balance is held centrally | Simple; one set of public books; nothing to lose but the card | A single point of failure and of power. A record of every purchase in one place is a tool of surveillance |
| **Many offices, federated** | Each region or council keeps its members' accounts, and the offices settle with each other, as banks do through a clearing house | Closer to the GIC's federalism ([17 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/17_Final_Considerations.md#f-centralism---federalism)); no single failure | The offices have to agree, and their books have to reconcile |
| **Stored on a card** | The balance sits on a chip, and the till deducts from it offline | Works without a network; less is known centrally | The card *is* the certificate. If it can be handed over, it circulates, and it can be lost or forged |
| **Paper certificates** | Marx's "theatre ticket"; Owen's labour notes | Needs no machines | Paper is a bearer instrument. Whoever holds it can spend it, so it passes from hand to hand and becomes money |

*My reading:* the line that matters is between an **account** and a **bearer token**. A certificate that is a record in an account can be made non-transferable; one that is an object, paper or a chip, can't, because whoever holds it can hand it on. So the certificate should live in an account, and a card should be only a key to it. Where the account is kept (one office or several) is a question of politics and resilience, not of transferability.

The cost of accounts is privacy. If every purchase is recorded against a name, the books know what everyone eats, reads and takes. The books don't need that. What the store needs is that the certificate was valid and is now cancelled; what the accounts need is how much of each product left the store. The two records can be kept apart, so the product is recorded without the buyer, and the buyer's account shows only a total spent.

### Does a blockchain help?

**How a blockchain works.** A blockchain is a ledger copied to many computers, with no single keeper. Transactions are collected into blocks. Each block carries a hash of the block before it, a short fingerprint that changes completely if any bit of the earlier block changes. So the blocks form a chain, and altering an old entry would mean rewriting every block after it. The computers agree on which block comes next by a consensus rule. In Bitcoin it is *proof of work*: "miners" compete to find a number that gives the block a hash below a target, which can only be done by guessing, at great cost in electricity, and the winner is paid in new coins. Other chains use *proof of stake*, where the right to add a block is given in proportion to coins held. An account is a pair of cryptographic keys: whoever holds the private key can spend what the account holds, and there is no one to appeal to if it is lost or stolen. Every transaction is public, under a pseudonym.

**What it is for.** It solves one problem: keeping a ledger that many parties agree on when they don't trust each other and won't trust any keeper ([crypto, above](#what-crypto-thought-was-new)).

*My reading.* That isn't this economy's problem. The GIC's books have a keeper, the public accounting office, answerable to the councils, and the books are open to all. Most of what a blockchain brings works against the design:

- **Transfer is its purpose.** A blockchain token exists to be sent from one key to another. Certificates exist not to be. Limiting transfers to "spent at a store" needs someone to say who is a store, which is a keeper again.
- **Proof of work burns labour.** It secures the ledger by wasting effort on purpose. In an economy whose measure is hours, that is the one thing it can't afford.
- **Public ledgers end privacy.** Everyone's spending is on show under a pseudonym, and pseudonyms are easy to link.
- **Keys are bearer tokens.** Whoever has the key has the account. A lost key is lost certificates, and a stolen or sold key is a transfer.

One part is useful: **the hash chain**. The public accounting office can publish its books as an append-only log, each entry chained to the one before, and let every council keep a copy. Then no entry can be changed afterwards without every copy showing it. That is tamper-evidence without mining, coins or anonymity, the same idea as the logs that certificate authorities on the web now publish. Several federated offices can also agree on one set of books with an ordinary consensus protocol among known parties. Neither needs a blockchain in the crypto sense.

### Paying for illicit goods

*Could someone using a direct transfer use their certificates for illicit drugs?* Not directly. A direct transfer only moves certificates from a household's account to a store, which cancels them ([Accounting](../plans/simulator/accounting.md#labour-certificates)). No certificate passes between units ([invariant 6](../plans/simulator/accounting.md#invariants)), or from one household to another. And there is a deeper reason: **in this economy a sale earns the seller nothing.** A unit's workers are credited for the hours they work, not for what the unit sells. So even a dealer who set up as a unit and took certificates through the store would gain nothing from it.

The ways round it are all indirect:

1. **Pay in goods.** Buy goods that are durable and wanted (alcohol, tobacco, electronics) and hand them to the dealer. The goods are the money, as cigarettes were in the camp. This is the easiest route, and the books see only an ordinary purchase. The store sees it only if one household buys far more of something than it can use.
2. **Buy for the dealer.** The dealer names what they want and the buyer buys it, or lends the dealer their card. A pooled household shares an account by design, so lending within a household can't be told from normal use; outside it, a card that needs its owner (a PIN, a face) makes this harder, not impossible.
3. **Ghost hours.** A unit records hours for the dealer, who never works there, and the dealer is paid in certificates; the unit's members are paid in drugs. In the books it is "claiming hours not worked": the unit's production time rises above its branch's average ([gap 45](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-simulation)).
4. **A sham household.** The dealer joins a pooled household and draws on its account. It shows only to the people who know who lives there.
5. **Medicines diverted.** Medical supplies are free from a public unit, so diverting opioids needs no certificates at all. It shows as stock missing at a stocktake, or as prescriptions that don't match patients.
6. **Through the outside.** TNS let a citizen abroad turn labour credits into foreign currency ([TNS ch. 10](../Documents/towards_a_new_socialism/10_Foreign_Trade.md#exchange-rates-tourism-and-black-markets)). Drugs can be bought there and smuggled home, or a supplier abroad paid. If such transfers can be made from home, they are a direct line to a cartel; TNS's own limit on what travellers take out (their budget 5) is the control.
7. **Old money, foreign notes, gold.** Where these are still held, they pay the dealer, and the dealer pays the importer. This is why the cancellation of old money matters for drugs as well as for counter-revolution.

The pattern is the same as everywhere in this note. The certificate itself is hard to misuse. Goods, labour and the outside's money are not, and the books show the misuse only where it leaves a trace in production times or stock.

## Sex work

Patrick asks whether it would be a public operational unit. *My reading* is no. In the GIC's categories ([10 a](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#a-two-forms-of-distribution)):

| Category | What it would mean | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Public** (general social work) | Society budgets sex as a need and supplies it free, like schools or clinics | No. A public unit supplies on demand within a budget; sex can't be made something anyone is obliged to supply. Sexual health services are public, as health is |
| **Productive** | A cooperative of sex workers under its own council, recording its hours and charging its production time, bought with certificates | Possible. It is the decriminalisation model (New Zealand's Prostitution Reform Act 2003, *not checked*) in labour-time form |
| **Off the books** | Paid in goods or a reinvented money | Where it goes if the councils prohibit or stigmatise it. An edge, like any other undeclared labour |

The Marxist tradition expected prostitution to wither with the economic dependence of women: Engels treats it as the counterpart of monogamous marriage founded on property (*The Origin of the Family*, 1884), and Kollontai's "Prostitution and Ways of Fighting It" (1921) as a social problem to be removed by economic independence, not by punishing the women (*not checked*). The GIC and TNS say nothing about it.

*My reading.* A labour-time economy reduces the economic compulsion: everyone who works has an equal claim per hour, and those unable to work are credited hours. It doesn't remove it. Under the plans as they stand, those who refuse work receive no certificates ([gap 48](../plans/simulator/gaps-in-the-brief.md#the-economics)), and anyone can want more than their hours buy. So whether sex work persists, and in which column, is partly a test of how well the equal hour does what the tradition hoped. Which column it belongs in is for the councils to decide. For the simulation it is out of scope.

## For the simulation

Nothing here changes a decision. Proposals, for Patrick to accept or not:

- **Care of disabled people** at home, paid as public work through a care unit ([gap 53](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics)).
- **Record unpaid housework beside the books**, as hours not credited, so the largest outside is visible ([gap 54](../plans/simulator/gaps-decided.md#the-economics)).
- **Add two forms of dishonesty to gap 45**: side exchange (households trading goods or services off the books) and unrecorded labour. Both need a check in the engine: an improbably *low* production time, as well as a high one.
- **Show the gate as the only door to means of production**, which is what makes reinvented money petty. A later scenario could open it to money, to show what happens.
- **Add goods as payment to gap 45**: a household buying far more of a durable good than it can use is the trace a store could look for.
- **Hash-chain the books.** The engine's books are already append-only ([Data model](../plans/simulator/data-model.md)); chaining each entry to the last would make a later change visible, and shows the one useful idea from blockchains.

## Open questions

- **Below-average production times.** The GIC investigate units above the average. Should a unit far below it be investigated too, and how far is "far"?
- **Housework.** Record it, credit it, or collectivise it (TNS's commune)? The [general social work note](general-social-work.md#unpaid-domestic-and-care-work) covers the arguments.
- **Gifts.** Where is the line between a gift, a swap and a market? *My reading:* not in the goods but in regularity. Occasional gifts are not production for exchange; making things regularly to swap is.
- **Did any council communist write on the black market?** Nothing was found in the *Fundamental Principles*.
- **Old money.** Should personal holdings convert into certificates at all, and if so, up to what limit? Who judges proof of origin?
- **Privacy.** Should the books record who bought what, or only what left the store?
