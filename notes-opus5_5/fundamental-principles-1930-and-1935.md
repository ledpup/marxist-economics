# The 1930 and 1935 editions of the *Fundamental Principles*

How the first edition of the *Fundamental Principles of Communist Production and Distribution* (German, 1930) differs from the second (Dutch, 1935), which is [the text in this repository](../Documents/fundamental_principles/README.md). The question that started it: the GIC says it had not read Marx's *Critique of the Gotha Programme* while it was doing the research. Are the book's quotations from the *Critique* therefore additions made in 1935?

This is a research note by Claude (Opus 5.5), written from a prompt by Patrick. Points marked *my reading* are interpretation, not something the sources say.

## Summary

- **No. The *Critique* is already quoted in 1930.** The first edition quotes it in two chapters (four passages), refers to it in three more, and quotes the "deductions" passage at length in the Epilogue. The admission that the GIC had not known the text is in that 1930 Epilogue too. The GIC finished its research, then found the *Critique* before the book went to press, and wrote it into the manuscript.
- **The 1935 edition moves the admission, which is what makes it look odd.** In 1930 it sits at the end, in an Epilogue about the *Critique*. In 1935 it is in the middle of the book ([10 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#f-the-marxist-solution)), after the *Critique* has already been quoted in chapters 3 and 7.
- **1935 adds some *Critique* quotations of its own:** the "certificate" sentence on its own in [9 i](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#i-fair-distribution), and the "altered circumstances" and "same principle" sentences again in [12 e](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#e-the-abolition-of-the-market-in-the-marxist-sense). It **drops** the 1930 Epilogue's long quotation of Marx's deductions, including the "funds for those unable to work".
- **The two editions are differently built books.** 1930 has 18 chapters and an Epilogue; 1935 has 17 chapters and an Appendix. The 1935 edition adds whole chapters on the workers' councils, anarchism and anarcho-syndicalism, and workers' control in Russia, and adds material from 1931 onwards. It cuts the 1930 edition's five chapters on the peasantry to one, and drops the 1930 chapter on "average social reproduction time" and depreciation.
- **The GIC's own word for the changes, "no fundamental changes" ([Foreword to the second edition](../Documents/fundamental_principles/00d_Foreword_to_the_Second_Edition.md)), understates them.** The economic model (the formula (P + C) + L, the payout factor, public operational units, accumulation, bookkeeping) is the same. The polemics around it, and the history, were largely rewritten.

| Structure | Preface ("In Place of a Preface"), 18 chapters, Epilogue | Objective, three forewords, 17 chapters, Appendix, Literature |

There was a third version in between. The [Foreword to the first edition](../Documents/fundamental_principles/00c_Foreword_to_the_First_Edition.md), dated June 1931, is the foreword to a Dutch edition serialised as a supplement to the GIC's *Persmateriaal* (PIC). It says that edition "is not identical with the German one". Some of what this note calls "1935" changes may date from 1931; the 1931 text was not compared.

**Cautions.**

- Both texts are translations, by different people, from different languages: Baker from German, Lueer from Dutch by way of German. Baker writes "GSU" (general social use) where Lueer writes "GSW" (general social work), "establishment" where Lueer writes "operational unit", and so on. Differences of wording prove nothing. This note compares structure, content and citations only.
- Baker replaced some of the GIC's references with English editions (for example "Progress Publishers, Moscow; 1978" for the *Critique*). His reference "[Critique of the Gotha Programme quoted in] Elementary Textbooks of Communism, Vol. 12, p. 16" (ch. III, note 2; PDF p. 69) is not an English edition, so it is presumably the GIC's own. *My reading:* it is a German edition in the series *Elementarbücher des Kommunismus*, which would fit the 1922 edition the Epilogue mentions. Not checked.
- The word counts below are rough: Baker's translation is wordier than Lueer's, and the marxists.org pages carry some headers and contents lists.

**What this means for the question.** Both editions say the research was finished before the GIC read the *Critique*. Both also quote it. The likeliest sequence (*my reading*): the GIC's discussions ran from about 1926 to 1929 (the 1931 foreword says "four years of discussion"); Jan Appel's draft and the GIC's analysis were done without the *Critique*; the GIC then found a German edition of it (1922 or 1928) and worked quotations into the manuscript before the 1930 printing, mainly where the argument already matched Marx. The admission is at the end in 1930, so the quotations come before it there too; the 1935 placement makes no difference to that.

**A correction for another note.** [Unemployment, dismissal and those unable to work](unemployment-and-inability-to-work.md) says "The GIC never quotes Marx's third deduction". That is true of the 1935 edition. The 1930 Epilogue quotes it in full ("Thirdly, funds for those unable to work, etc."), and draws only one conclusion from it: social costs come from "a simple deduction from the individually consumed product", with no "mountain of statistics".

## What the 1935 edition adds

- **Russia, in detail.** "Supreme Economic Council" appears 3 times in Baker's 1930 text and 22 times in 1935; Lenin once against 28 times; Trotsky, Kerensky, Kronstadt, the NEP and the Russian works councils not at all in 1930. Most of this is in [14a](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14a_The_Control_of_Operational_Life.md), [12 a–b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#a-with-the-bolsheviks-the-supreme-economic-council-distributes-the-product-of-society) and the Appendix. *My reading:* by 1935 the argument against the Bolsheviks is historical as well as theoretical.
- **Anarchism and syndicalism.** The 1930 edition criticises "free communism" (Sébastien Faure) in one section. The 1935 edition gives it a chapter, with the Spanish CNT, the Dutch syndicalists and *La Révolution prolétarienne*, all from 1931.
- **The workers' councils as the starting point** ([ch. 1](../Documents/fundamental_principles/01_Starting_Points.md)), and the slogan in the [Objective](../Documents/fundamental_principles/00a_Objective.md).
- **Later events.** The five-year plan, the summaries in *Kampfsignal* and *Council Correspondence*, and dates up to January 1935.
- **Inequality of equal right** ([9 i](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#i-fair-distribution)): the 1930 edition says only that all workers have "an equal right" to the social stock (ch. X.2).

## What the 1930 edition has that 1935 does not

- **The long *Critique* quotation**, with Marx's six deductions, and the GIC's comment that Marx saw communist economy "as a closed, self-contained process" whose measure "must proceed out of production itself".
- **Average social reproduction time and depreciation** (ch. XII). The 1930 edition argues that "socially necessary labour" is too loose a category, and that each production group computes an average *reproduction* time, so obsolete plant is handled through the productivity factor. The 1935 edition talks only of average production time.
- **The peasantry at length:** the Russian and Hungarian agrarian revolutions, and the German revolution of 1918–23 in the countryside (chs. XVI–XVIII).
- **Some authors drop out or shrink:** Pannekoek on "socialisation" (ch. I, note 10) and Bukharin's critique of Böhm-Bawerk (Epilogue) are gone. Neurath is named 18 times in Baker's 1930 text and 6 times in 1935. Erich Horn's "negative system" moves from the Epilogue to [4 a](../Documents/fundamental_principles/04_Progress_in_the_Formulation_of_the_Problem.md#a-communism-as-a-negative-system).
