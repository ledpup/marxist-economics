# Unemployment, dismissal and those unable to work

Whether the *[Fundamental Principles](../Documents/fundamental_principles/README.md)* deals with being out of work: being dismissed, finding a new place, living without work, and being too ill, injured or old to work. Then what Marx, Pannekoek, Cockshott and Cottrell and the present-day labour-time circle say, and what the question looks like at today's scale: mass unemployment, the informal "planet of slums", and "bullshit jobs".

This is a research note by Claude (Opus 5.5), written from a prompt by Patrick. Points marked *my reading* or *my illustration* are interpretation or construction, not something the sources say. Quotations keep the translation's American spelling ("labor").

## Summary

- **The GIC barely deals with any of it.** "Unemployment" appears in the GIC's own voice once, in the [Objective](../Documents/fundamental_principles/00a_Objective.md), as a symptom of capitalism. The unemployed, the sick and invalids appear once more, in brackets, inside a quotation from Varga that the GIC rejects ([9 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#g-the-distribution-of-the-means-of-production)). There is no section on hiring, dismissal, changing jobs, pensions, sick pay or disability.
- **What the book does settle:**
  - Labour certificates are issued for hours recorded in an operational unit ([9 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#c-the-consumption-money)). Anyone outside a unit's books gets none.
  - The GIC rejects direction of labour outright: its chapter on Trotsky's "labour duty" is the strongest passage on the subject ([12 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#b-the-supreme-economic-council-distributes-the-labor-power)).
  - It rejects closures decided "from above" that leave workforces "on the pavement" ([9 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#g-the-distribution-of-the-means-of-production)).
  - It rejects dismissal to a subsistence minimum as a punishment ([9 d](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#d-the-consumption-money-by-leichter)).
  - Health care and "social institutions" are free GSW services ([10 a](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#a-two-forms-of-distribution)).
  - A "general fund" covers disaster victims ([13 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#g-the-general-fund)).
- **"Those who do not work will not eat"** is in the book ([ch. 16](../Documents/fundamental_principles/16_The_Economic_Dictatorship_of_the_Proletariat.md)), but aimed at the propertied classes, not at the sick or the jobless.
- **The GIC never quotes Marx's third deduction**, the "funds for those unable to work" in the *Critique of the Gotha Programme*, even though it says its GSW solution matches Marx's ([10 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#f-the-marxist-solution)). Pannekoek's *Workers' Councils* (1947) and Cockshott and Cottrell's *Towards a New Socialism* (1993) both fill the gap explicitly.
- **The arithmetic is easy.** Non-workers' consumption is one more deduction before the payout factor (FIC) is worked out, like the GSW budget. The harder questions are institutional: who admits you to a unit, who removes you, what happens when your unit shrinks. The book is silent on all three.
- **On scale:** the 1930s had mass unemployment too; the GIC opens with it. What is new is the informal working class of the global South, which Mike Davis puts at about a billion people. The GIC's scheme assumes an industrial production apparatus to be taken over. Most of that population has none to take over.

## What the GIC says

### Unemployment

The book's first sentence ([Objective](../Documents/fundamental_principles/00a_Objective.md)):

> The development of capitalism is leading to increasingly serious crises, reflected in ever-increasing unemployment and an ever-deeper disruption of the production system, leaving millions of workers out of production and starving to death.

That is unemployment as a cause of revolution, not as a problem for the new society. After this the word appears only in other people's mouths:

- **Leichter** ([9 d](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#d-the-consumption-money-by-leichter)). An appointed manager who fails "can be removed without further ado, just like a capitalist manager". "He will then only receive the minimum income guaranteed by society if he is 'unemployed', or he will be used in a correspondingly lower and therefore, worse paid position." The GIC's comment: "It speaks for Leichter to call it one of the most severe punishments when someone is brought to the subsistence level on a nutritional basis." The GIC objects to the subsistence minimum and to one-man responsibility. It does not say what should happen to someone who does fail.
- **Varga** ([9 h](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#h-the-pricing-policy-in-hungary)). Soviet Hungary priced its goods above cost "to maintain the above-mentioned unproductive strata of the population", to which the GIC adds in brackets: "This refers to soldiers, civil servants, teachers, unemployed, sick, invalids - GIC". The GIC attacks the *method*, a mark-up on prices, and replaces it with the payout factor. It never comes back to the unemployed, the sick and invalids by name. Teachers reappear as GSW workers; the other three groups do not.

### Dismissal and closure

The book has no rule on who may join or leave an operational unit. What it gives:

- **Who runs a unit.** "Each operational unit thus forms a collective legal entity which is responsible to the community for its management" ([14b b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)). Decisions are made "as an operational unit" in the general meeting of the workforce, as in the councils of 1917–23 ([14 d](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14a_The_Control_of_Operational_Life.md#d-the-destruction-of-workers-control-by-the-bolsheviks)). So if anyone dismisses, it is the workforce, not a manager: *my reading*.
- **Closure from above, rejected.** Under the Bolsheviks the raw material centres concentrated industry "by simply cutting off factories which one wanted to bring to a standstill 'from above' from the supply of materials, which then caused the workforce of the factory to hit the pavement". Workers resisted a process "just as fatal for them in its economic consequences as it was under capitalism" ([9 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#g-the-distribution-of-the-means-of-production)).
- **Closure from below, by the books.** A unit whose actual production time exceeds the social average "cannot reproduce" itself, and "production comes to a standstill". The average is "a relentless 'controller'" ([14b b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)). The book does not follow the workforce of such a unit any further.
- **Whole branches shrinking.** The GIC expects this on a large scale. Luxury industries "are coming to a standstill or must be directed to the needs of the workers as quickly as possible"; the first stage "will, therefore, be characterized by the strong growth of some industries and the shrinking of others", a transformation "that will certainly not be without problems and inconveniences" ([12 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#f-the-orientation-of-production-to-the-needs)). This is the book's biggest unacknowledged movement of labour.

### Finding another place: no direction of labour

The clearest position in the book is a negative one. In [12 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#b-the-supreme-economic-council-distributes-the-labor-power) the GIC quotes Trotsky:

> »*... the working class must not lead a nomadic life. It must be moved, distributed, and detached, just like the soldiers*.«

and the Committee for the General Labor Duty (December 1919), which sent the demobilised skilled worker "with the workbook in hand ... where his presence is required" and could order "the transfer of labor from one operational unit to another". The GIC's verdict: in this system "all *individual* freedom has ceased", and central control of production means the centre "must have access to the human material". It calls the result the "enslavement of the working class".

So the GIC's society cannot assign people to jobs. It follows that people find places themselves, and units take them on: something like the capitalist labour market, minus the wage, the boss and the threat of destitution. The book never describes it: *my reading*.

One constraint does follow from the text. Once the production plan is adopted, units "must not exceed their production budgets" ([14b b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/14b_The_Control_of_Operational_Life_II.md#b-control-under-communism)). The budget is (f + c) + l, and l is hours of labour. A unit can't simply take on everyone who turns up; its hours are planned. The 10 % expansion right in [13 c](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#c-the-general-rule-for-the-expansion-of-the-operational-unit) covers means of production and raw materials, not workers. *My reading:* whether there is a place for you depends on the plan, and the plan is drawn up by the councils. The book leaves how it is drawn up outside its scope ([7 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/07_The_Communist_Production.md#b-the-production-time)).

### "Those who do not work will not eat"

[Chapter 16](../Documents/fundamental_principles/16_The_Economic_Dictatorship_of_the_Proletariat.md):

> The introduction of communist economic life means nothing other than the abolition of wage labor, the implementation of the *equal right* of all producers to the social stocks. It means the abolition of all privileges of certain classes. Communist business life does not give anyone the right to enrich himself at the expense of labor. Those who do not work will not eat.

The target is the shareholder, as in [9 i](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#i-fair-distribution): "no one can indeed eat with idleness, just as shareholders collect dividends". The saying is older than socialism. In 2 Thessalonians 3:10 it is "if any would not work, neither should he eat": the unwilling, not the unable. Lenin wrote in *[The State and Revolution](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1917/staterev/ch05.htm)* (1917, ch. 5) that under socialism "'He who does not work shall not eat' is already realized". The 1918 Soviet constitution made it a state motto, alongside work as "the duty of every citizen" (Article 18). The GIC uses the phrase in the same anti-class sense but without saying how far it reaches.

### Illness, injury, old age

There is no provision for the individual consumption of anyone who can't work. What the book does give:

- **Free care.** GSW units include "health care, education ... all types of cultural and social institutions" ([10 a](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#a-two-forms-of-distribution)). A hospital or a care home costs the patient nothing. Food, clothing and rent for a person who can't work are a different matter.
- **The growing free sector.** "Food supply, personal transport ... housing service" are expected to become free ([10 h](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#h-the-growth-process-of-communism)). Everything that moves to the GSW sector reaches workers and non-workers alike. *My reading:* this is the GIC's real, unstated answer. As basic needs become free, the problem of the non-worker shrinks. But it is a destination, not a provision for year one.
- **The general fund** for "major floods, hurricanes, peat fires, etc., where the victims are dependent on the help of a 'private charity'. Under communism, this type of hardship will have to be borne by the whole of society" ([13 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#g-the-general-fund)). The principle, that hardship is a social cost carried by the payout factor, applies just as well to illness and injury. The book applies it only to disasters.
- **Dependants.** The worker with five children and the single worker get the same per hour, and the GIC calls this "a great injustice in practical consumption" that it accepts on political grounds ([9 i](../Documents/fundamental_principles/09_Average_Working_Hour_as_Basis_of_Consumption.md#i-fair-distribution)). Children and non-working partners consume out of a worker's certificates. Nothing is said about the old.

In [7 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/07_The_Communist_Production.md#f-the-value-of-labor-power-in-life-circumstances-communism) the GIC quotes Leichter on the cost of replacing a skilled worker "if the former carrier of this labor power is no longer able to work". It treats this only as an argument about pay differentials.

### Useless work and the length of the working day

The GIC anticipates part of Graeber's argument. The market leaves the proletariat "a production apparatus which unproductively squanders at least half of the labor power" ([12 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/12_The_Abolition_of_the_Market.md#f-the-orientation-of-production-to-the-needs)). And the working day is a variable decided socially: expansion might mean "the working time would have to be extended by 5 hours per week", and "this speed determines the length of the working day" ([13 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#b-the-expansion-of-production-is-always-at-the-expense-of-consumption)). It never runs the argument the other way: shorter hours, so that work freed from useless jobs is shared among everyone able to do it.

## Marx

**The third deduction.** In the *[Critique of the Gotha Programme](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1875/gotha/ch01.htm)*, before anything is divided among individuals, society deducts "first, the general costs of administration"; "second, that which is intended for the common satisfaction of needs, such as schools, health services, etc."; "third, funds for those unable to work, etc., in short, for what is included under so-called official poor relief today". The GIC's GSW budget is the first two. The third has no counterpart in the book. The [extract in this repository](../Documents/Critique_of_the_Gotha_Programme-certificates.md) starts after this passage.

**Sharing the work.** *[Capital](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch17.htm)*, vol. 1, ch. 17: the time society must devote to material production is shorter, and free time greater, "in proportion as the work is more and more evenly divided among all the able-bodied members of society, and as a particular class is more and more deprived of the power to shift the natural burden of labour from its own shoulders to those of another layer of society". Marx's answer to unemployment in a planned society is shorter hours for everyone, not benefits for some.

**The surplus population.** *[Capital](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch25.htm#S4)*, vol. 1, ch. 25, section 4, sorts the "relative surplus population" into the *floating* (workers laid off and taken on again), the *latent* (the rural population waiting to move to the towns), and the *stagnant* ("extremely irregular employment"). Below them is pauperism, which includes "those unable to work", "the mutilated, the sickly, the widows, etc.", "the victims of industry". Below that are "vagabonds, criminals, prostitutes": the lumpenproletariat proper.

**A note on terms.** For Marx and Engels "lumpenproletariat" means the bottom layer only, "the 'dangerous class', the social scum" of the *[Manifesto](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/ch01.htm)*. The unemployed and the slum workers of today are in Marx's terms mostly the *stagnant* and *latent* surplus population, and Davis calls them the "informal proletariat". The distinction matters here because the GIC's scheme counts on them as workers.

## Others who answer the question

### Pannekoek, *Workers' Councils* (1947)

Pannekoek is the council communist closest to the GIC, and he says what the GIC leaves out. From Part I, "The Task", [§4 "Social Organization"](https://www.marxists.org/archive/pannekoe/1947/workers-councils.htm):

> Children and old people will receive their share of the produce without corresponding achievements. People incapable of work must be sustained; and especially in the first time there will be a large number of human wrecks left by the former capitalist world.

On the duty to work, in the same section: "The old popular saying that whoever does not work shall not eat, expresses an instinctive feeling of justice", and "everyone's duty to take part in the production in accordance with his forces and capacities, everyone's right to enjoy his adequate part of the collective product". In "times of reconstruction ... the right of consumption will be coupled to the performance of work". On work-sharing (§8 "Growth"): drawing in those formerly wasted "in the bungling of petty trade" "now helps to lower the necessary hours of labor for all".

On finding a place he offers an assurance rather than a mechanism (§6 "Difficulties"): "everybody who is willing to co-operate in the community of labor will be welcome and will find the place and the task appropriate to his capacities". And on unemployment (§1 "Labor"): it is "the worst scourge of the working class under capitalism. It is inherent in capitalism".

### Cockshott and Cottrell, *[Towards a New Socialism](../PDFs/TOWARDS%20A%20NEW%20SOCIALISM.pdf)* (1993)

The most explicit modern scheme, and the one that names all three groups.

- **Work guaranteed.** "In a socialist economy the unemployment sanction is quite deliberately removed. Work is guaranteed for all" (p. 33). The plan "should be designed to use up the available labour resources, without either excess demand or excess supply" (p. 31).
- **Tokens for non-workers.** "A part of the plan's total personal consumption allowance must be made available to non-workers--retired people, the disabled, people moving between jobs." The tokens "cannot simply be 'printed up' (this would be inflationary); rather, they must be taxed away from the workers and transferred to the non-workers" (p. 91). The non-workers they list are "soldiers, pensioners, children, the sick, etc." (p. 32).
- **Direction of labour.** They note it exists under capitalism in disguise. Benefit offices "remove benefits from people who turn down an offer of work. It is not called direction of labour but the effect is the same" (p. 31). Shortages in particular trades are met by training, by better conditions, or as a last resort by a "scarcity rent" financed from taxation.
- **Without the firing sanction**, they propose an economy-wide grading of labour (A, B, C) to keep effort up (p. 33). The GIC would reject this as a wage differential.

Their unemployment chapter (ch. 1, pp. 16–18) argues that unemployment under capitalism "is the regulator of exploitation" and that "the poverty of the unemployed is a gateway to poverty for the employed".

### The labour-time circle today

The Initiative Demokratische Arbeitszeitrechnung (IDA) [FAQ](https://arbeitszeitrechnung.org/en/faqs-2/) gives short answers in the GIC tradition:

- "Those who cannot work are provided for by the public sector", and the FIC is "intended to make certain goods and services freely available to all, which will in any case first provide for all those who are unable to work."
- On where people work: "People regulate that among themselves. On the one hand, very large companies in particular must always provide information about whether they need additional workers; on the other hand, it should be possible for everyone to change jobs and places of residence at any time."
- On the unwilling: "Perhaps, at some point, a society can be generous enough to also provide for all those who are unwilling to work."
- On useless work: "many unnecessary jobs that are only 'meaningful' under capitalism (advertising, financial services, etc.) would be eliminated", and "the less effort is required for the necessary work, the more time is freely available to everyone".

An Angry Workers critique ([reposted by workers.coop](https://www.workers.coop/thoughts-on-the-fundamental-principles-of-communist-production-and-distribution-long-read/), 30 July 2025) asks a different question. Whether individual labour-time accounting imposes an "individual coercion to work" seems less interesting to its author than whether the scheme is "avoiding the issue of coercion by transferring it onto an economic dynamic". A worker turned away by every unit's planned budget meets that dynamic, not a person: *my reading*.

## The arithmetic

### Supporting non-workers through the payout factor

*My illustration*, using the GIC's own figures from [10 g](../Documents/fundamental_principles/10_The_General_Social_Work.md#g-the-payout-factor). Without non-workers:

FIC = (L − (F<sub>p</sub> + C<sub>p</sub>)) / (L + L<sub>p</sub>) = (600 − 58) / 650 = 0.83

Write X for the consumption society sets aside for those who can't work: pensions, sick pay, disability support, and support while between units. It comes off the top line, like the GSW units' means of production, but adds nothing to the bottom line, because nobody works those hours:

FIC = (L − (F<sub>p</sub> + C<sub>p</sub>) − X) / (L + L<sub>p</sub>)

With X = 65 million hours (a tenth of the hours worked):

FIC = (542 − 65) / 650 = 477 / 650 = 0.73

A 40-hour week now buys 29.4 hours of products instead of 33.2. Total claims, 0.734 × 650 + 65 = 542 million hours, still equal the product left for consumption. Like the GSW budget, X is a public decision made at the councils' congresses, not a matter of price policy. It fits the GIC's rules exactly; the book just doesn't include it.

The non-workers' share could be paid as certificates, as Cockshott and Cottrell propose, or as free goods. The second fits the GIC's growth process better: if food and housing move into the GSW sector, X is mostly absorbed into F<sub>p</sub> + C<sub>p</sub> + L<sub>p</sub> and nobody needs a separate allowance.

### Benefits or shorter hours

Suppose a tenth more people are able and willing to work, but the plan needs no more than 650 million hours.

- **Pay them to stay out.** X rises and everyone else's FIC falls, as above.
- **Share the hours (Marx, Pannekoek).** Everyone works about 36.4 hours instead of 40, the FIC stays at 0.83, and each person gets 0.83 × 36.4 = 30.2 hours of products instead of 33.2.

The total consumed is the same either way. What differs is who works and who decides about whom. The real gain comes only if the extra hours are *added*, not shared: if there are unmet needs and means of production to meet them. That is the situation of most of the world's surplus population today.

## Then and now

### The 1930s

Patrick's guess was that mass unemployment in the 1930s was mostly a Depression effect. It was. The GIC wrote in the middle of it, and the book's first sentence is about it. German registered unemployment passed six million in early 1932. In the Netherlands, where the GIC worked, figures for the mid-1930s range from about 480,000 in 1936 ([Michielsen](https://www.marxists.org/nederlands/michielsen/1976/geschied/51.htm), who gives 36.3 % of manual workers) to a peak of about 600,000 in 1935 ([Canon van Nederland](https://canonvannederland.nl/en/crisisjaren)). The sources disagree, and not everyone was registered.

What the GIC treated as a separate question was the equivalent of Marx's *latent* surplus population: the peasantry. [Chapter 15](../Documents/fundamental_principles/15_The_Introduction_of_Communism_in_Agriculture.md) expects industrialised farms to keep the same books as factories, and treats small peasants and the "semi-proletariat" as a political problem for the transition.

### Today

- **Unemployment and the jobs gap.** The ILO's *[Employment and Social Trends 2026](https://www.ilo.org/publications/flagship-reports/employment-and-social-trends-2026)* (January 2026) puts global unemployment at 4.9 %, about 186 million people. The wider "jobs gap", which counts people who want work but are not counted as unemployed, is about 408 million. About 2.1 billion people work informally, and more than a quarter of young people are not in employment, education or training. *These figures are from press coverage of the report*: check them against the report itself.
- **The planet of slums.** Mike Davis, *Planet of Slums* (2006), builds on UN-Habitat's *The Challenge of Slums* (2003), which counted close to a billion slum dwellers. Davis puts the global informal working class at about a billion people too. His chapter "A Surplus Humanity?" argues that this class was not foreseen by classical Marxism, and that much of it is permanently outside formal employment, not a reserve army waiting to be called up. *Wording from summaries, not checked against the book.*
- **Bullshit jobs.** David Graeber's 2013 essay and his book *Bullshit Jobs* (2018) define a job "so completely pointless, unnecessary, or pernicious that even the employee cannot justify its existence". He leaned on a 2015 YouGov poll in which 37 % of British workers said their job made no meaningful contribution to the world. Soffia, Wood and Burchell, using the European Working Conditions Survey ([*Work, Employment and Society*, 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09500170231175771)), found the share who see their own job as useless is much lower, about 5 %, and falling. Graeber's own category, though, includes jobs the holder thinks useful but which are not. The GIC's "at least half of the labor power" squandered is a claim of that second kind: about the system, not about how workers see their jobs.

### What the GIC's scheme implies at this scale (*my reading*)

1. **The arithmetic is not the obstacle.** Supporting non-workers is one term in the payout factor. In an industrial economy where useless work is cut and the free sector grows, it is affordable.
2. **The scheme counts on taking over a production apparatus.** Its whole mechanism starts from operational units that already exist: workers occupy them, keep their books in hours and pass their products on. The informal proletariat mostly works with almost no means of production: street trade, piecework at home, casual labour. There is little for it to take over.
3. **Bringing it in is "special accumulation".** Building the units that would employ it is the kind of multi-year public work that [13 f](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#f-special-accumulation) charges to the GSW budget, and so to everyone's payout factor. At world scale this means workers in industrial regions consuming less while units are built elsewhere. The GIC never considers a society larger than one industrial country. See [LETS, Parecon and the GIC](lets-parecon-and-one-country.md) on the GIC and the outside world.
4. **Useless jobs are the easier half.** Cutting them frees hours, and the GIC's rule that every hour counts equally means nobody loses status by moving from a useless job to a useful one. But someone has to admit them to a useful unit, and the plan has to have room for them.

## What the book leaves open

- **Admission and removal.** Who decides that a person joins or leaves a unit, and on what grounds: the general meeting, the works council, or a rule set by congress.
- **Movement between units.** How someone leaving a shrinking unit finds a place in a growing one, given that units must stay within their planned labour budget.
- **Support between units.** Whether someone between units receives anything, and from which fund.
- **Inability to work.** Pensions, sick pay and disability: Marx's third deduction, which the book doesn't mention.
- **Refusal to work.** Whether the able but unwilling are simply left without certificates, as "those who do not work will not eat" implies, or supported, as IDA hopes society may one day be "generous enough" to do.
- **Hours.** Whether falling demand for labour is met by shorter hours for all or by fewer people working. The book treats the length of the working day as a social decision ([13 b](../Documents/fundamental_principles/13_The_Expansion_of_Production.md#b-the-expansion-of-production-is-always-at-the-expense-of-consumption)) but never in this direction.
